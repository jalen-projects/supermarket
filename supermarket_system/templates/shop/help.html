Cash out a customer (complete the payment)

  1. Open New sale (till).
  2. Scan each item, or type part of its name and press Enter. It drops into the list. Scan the same item twice and the quantity becomes 2, or type the quantity straight into the box.
  3. Look at the big TOTAL on the right. That is what the customer owes.
  4. Type what the customer handed you into Cash received. If they gave the exact money, tap Exact money. If they gave notes, tap the notes — 10,000 then 5,000 — and it adds them up for you.
  5. The Change box turns green and shows what to hand back. If it turns red, they have not given you enough yet.
  6. Press the green CASH OUT — complete payment button, or just press F9.

That one button does everything at once: it takes the money, prints the receipt, and removes the goods from your stock. There is nothing else to press afterwards.

The receipt did not print? The sale is still saved — look for the green line under the button and click Open the receipt again. You can also find it any time under Receipts.
Paying later, or paying part of it? Choose the customer's name at the top of the payment box first, set Paid by to Credit, and cash out. The balance then shows against that customer, and you can see everyone who owes you under Customers. Without picking a name, there is nobody to chase.

Put a new product into the system

  1. Products → Add a product.
  2. Type the name. Click into the barcode box and scan the item if it has a barcode — the scanner types it for you. No barcode? Leave it empty; you will find the item by name at the till.
  3. Choose the category (Beverages, Soap…) and the measurement (piece, kg, crate).
  4. Put in the buying price (what it cost you) and the selling price (what the customer pays). Both are needed, or your profit report cannot be right.
  5. Fill in How many do you have right now with what is already on the shelf. Add the expiry date if it has one.
  6. Press Save product. It can be sold immediately.

Adding many at once? Use Save and add another and it keeps the form open in front of you.

There are three different ways stock goes up, and each is right in its own place: when adding a new product (what is on the shelf today), Purchases (a delivery that arrived), and Stock take (counting shelves you have never entered).

Record the goods that are already in the shop

This is what a stock take is for. It is also how you find out, later on, what has gone missing.

  1. Stock take (count the shelves) in the menu.
  2. Pick one category — one shelf, one aisle. Do not try to count the whole shop in one go; save each shelf as you finish it.
  3. Walk the shelf. For each item, type what you actually find into the Counted box.
  4. The Difference column fills in as you type, so a wrong number is obvious while you are still standing there.
  5. Skip any row you did not look at. Empty is not zero — leaving it empty means "I did not count this", and nothing about it changes.
  6. Name what you counted, then press Save the count & correct the stock.

The system does the arithmetic. Anything extra goes onto the books; anything missing comes off. You then get a sheet showing every item that did not match and what the gap is worth — that is the page to read.

Products not on the system at all yet? Add them first (see above) and put the quantity in as you add each one. A stock take can only count things it knows the name of.

Record goods that came with no invoice number

Leave the box empty. It was never required.

  1. Purchases → Receive a delivery.
  2. Leave Invoice or delivery note number empty.
  3. Do not know the supplier either? Leave that as Not recorded / bought for cash.
  4. Add a line for each item: quantity, what you paid for one, and the expiry date if it has one.
  5. Press Receive into stock now.

Every delivery gets its own number from the system — GRN-00001 and so on — so it is still fully tracked whether or not the supplier gave you paperwork. If the invoice turns up later, open the delivery and type the number in then.

Delete a product

Products, find the item, press Remove. The screen then tells you which of two things is about to happen, before you confirm.

  • It really is deleted if it has never been delivered, sold or counted. That is the typing mistake — the same soap entered twice — and it goes for good.
  • It is taken off sale instead if it has any history. It vanishes from the till and from your product list, but every receipt and every report stays exactly as it was.

The second one is not the system being stubborn. That product's name is printed on receipts customers are holding and it is inside last month's profit figure. Erasing it would change what those receipts add up to, and then your books could never be trusted again.

Changed your mind? Products → the Not for sale tab → open it → Put it back on sale.

Fix a mistake

Charged the wrong thing, receipt already printed Receipts → open it → Void. Every item goes back onto the shelf. Then ring the sale up again properly.
Typed a delivery in wrongly If it is still a draft, open it and press Delete draft. If it was already received, the goods are on the shelf — correct the quantities with a stock take.
Goods expired, broke, or were stolen Open the product → Adjust stock, or use Write off on the batch. Say why — that note is what you read back later.
The shelf does not match the system Do a stock take of that shelf. It corrects the figures and records the gap at the same time.
Wrong price on an item Products → Edit. Past receipts keep the price that was actually charged.

Use the system on a second computer

Yes, and they share the same data. One computer runs the system; the others just open a browser and type in an address. Nothing is installed on them and it is still completely offline.
