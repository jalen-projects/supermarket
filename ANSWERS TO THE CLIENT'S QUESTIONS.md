## 0. Let the system show you

Before reading any of this: sign in, and when it offers to **show you round**, say yes.

The tour opens each real screen in turn and points at the exact box or button being
talked about — cashing out, entering a product, counting a shelf, a delivery with no
invoice, removing a product, and your second computer.

Press **Escape** to stop at any point. To run it again, or if you said "not now", go to
**How do I…?** at the bottom of the menu and press **Start the tour**.

Everything below is the same thing in writing, for when you would rather read it.

## 1. How to cash out (complete the payment)

**Till → scan the items → type what the customer gave you → press the green button.**

1. Open **New sale (till)**.
2. Scan each item, or type part of the name and press **Enter**.
3. Look at the big **TOTAL**. That is what the customer owes.
4. Type what they handed you into **Cash received** — or:
   - tap **Exact money** if they gave the exact amount;
   - tap the notes themselves — *10,000* then *5,000* — and it adds them up.
5. The **Change** box turns green and shows what to hand back. Red means they
   have not given you enough yet.
6. Press **CASH OUT — complete payment**, or just press **F9**.

That single button does everything: takes the money, prints the receipt, and
removes the goods from your stock. There is nothing else to press.

**If the receipt does not print,** the sale is still saved. A green line appears
under the button with a link to open the receipt again, and it is always under
**Receipts**.

**If somebody is paying later,** pick their name at the top of the payment box
first and set *Paid by* to **Credit**. The balance then shows against that
customer under **Customers**. Without picking a name there is nobody to chase.

## 2. How to enter products in the system

**Products → Add a product.**

Name, barcode (scan it, or leave empty for loose goods), category, measurement,
buying price, selling price.

Then the box that was missing: **"How many do you have right now?"** Put in what
is on the shelf today, and the expiry date if it has one. Press save, and the
item is on sale immediately.

Adding many at once? **Save and add another** keeps the form open in front of you.

> Stock goes up in three different places, and each is right in its own case:
> **when adding a new product** (what is on the shelf today), **Purchases** (a
> delivery that arrived), **Stock take** (counting shelves you never entered).

## 3. How to delete a product

**Products → find it → Remove.** The screen then tells you which of two things is
about to happen, before you confirm anything.

**It really is deleted** if the product has never been delivered, sold or counted.
That is the typing mistake — the same soap entered twice — and it goes for good.

**It is taken off sale instead** if it has any history. It disappears from the
till and from your product list, but every receipt and every report stays exactly
as it is.

That second one is not the system being difficult. That product's name is printed
on receipts your customers are holding, and it is inside last month's profit
figure. Erasing it would change what those receipts add up to — and once that can
happen, none of your books can be trusted.

Changed your mind? **Products → the "Not for sale" tab → open it → Put it back on
sale.**

## 4. How to record products with no invoice number

**Leave the box empty.** It was never required — nothing said so, which is the
real fault, and the screen now says it plainly.

**Purchases → Receive a delivery**, then:

- **Invoice or delivery note number** — leave it empty.
- **Supplier** — if you do not know it either, leave it as
  *"Not recorded / bought for cash"*. This one used to be compulsory, which is
  what actually blocked you. It is not any more.
- Add a line per item: quantity, what you paid for one, expiry date if it has one.
- **Receive into stock now**.

Every delivery gets its own number from the system — GRN-00001 and so on — so the
goods are fully tracked whether or not anyone gave you paperwork. If the invoice
turns up later, open the delivery and type the number in then.

**Typed a delivery in wrongly?** While it is still a draft, open it and press
**Delete draft**. Once it has been received the goods are on the shelf and may
already be sold, so correct it with a stock take instead.

## 5. How to do stock taking — the goods already in the supermarket

This was the biggest gap, and it now has a screen of its own:
**Stock take (count the shelves)**.

1. Pick **one category** — one shelf, one aisle. Do not try to count the whole
   supermarket in one sitting; save each shelf as you finish it. You can do
   Beverages in the morning and Soap after lunch, and nothing is lost.
2. Walk the shelf. Type what you **actually find** into the **Counted** box.
3. The **Difference** column fills in as you type, so a wrong number is obvious
   while you are still standing there — not next month.
4. **Leave a row empty if you did not count it.** Empty is *not* zero. Empty means
   "I did not look at this one", and nothing about it changes.
5. Name what you counted and press **Save the count & correct the stock**.

The system does the arithmetic. Anything extra goes onto the books; anything
missing comes off. You then get a sheet showing every item that did not match and
**what the gap is worth in shillings**.

That sheet is the point of the whole exercise. Once your shelves are on the
system, a stock take stops being data entry and becomes the thing that tells you
where your money is going — goods sold without being rung up, breakages, or theft.
Do one shelf a week and you will know your shop better than the books ever showed
you.

> **Products not on the system at all yet?** Add them first (section 2), putting
> the quantity in as you add each one. A stock take can only count things it
> already knows the name of.

## 6. Can two or more computers use the system at the same time?

**Yes — and they share exactly the same data. It stays completely offline.**

You said two computers. Two is comfortable, and everything below is written for that.

There is a new page for this: **Other computers** in the menu, under Setup. It
shows you the exact address to type, because that address depends on your router
and can change.

### How it works

One computer — the office one, or the main till — is the **server**. It is the
only computer the system is installed on, and it holds the only copy of your data.

Every other computer simply opens a browser (Chrome) and types in an address like
`http://192.168.1.14:8000/`. **Nothing is installed on them at all.**

That is the whole design, and the reason for it matters: if a sale is rung up on
till 2, the stock drops on till 1 and in the office **immediately**, because there
is only ever one set of books. Two separate copies of the system would be two
separate shops that disagree with each other by lunchtime, and no amount of
copying files at closing time would fix it.

### What you need

- The computers on the **same network** — the same router, cable or wi-fi.
- **No internet connection at any point.** The router does not even need one.
- The server switched on and running the system before the other tills can use it.
- One thing done once on the server: right-click **`ALLOW OTHER TILLS.bat`** and
  choose **Run as administrator**. Windows Firewall blocks the other tills until
  you do, and this is nearly always the reason it "does not work" the first time.

Then, on the other computer, open Chrome, type the address from the **Other
computers** page, and bookmark it. That is all.

### What you should know before relying on it

- **The server holds everything.** The other tills store nothing. Back up on the
  server, and keep taking that backup to a flash disk.
- **If the server goes off, every till stops.** That is the price of having one
  honest set of books. Put the server on the same power protection as the fridges;
  a small UPS on it is worth the money.
- **A receipt printer prints where it is plugged in.** Each till needs its own.
- **This is right for three or four computers in one shop, not fifty**, and not
  for a second branch across town. A chain of branches is a different system and
  a separate conversation.
- **Everyone must have their own login.** Anyone on your network who knows the
  address reaches the sign-in page, which is fine inside a shop — but it is one
  more reason cashiers stay cashiers. A cashier account cannot see your buying
  prices or your profit.

### Under the bonnet, for the record

The database was switched to **WAL mode**, so one cashier saving a sale no longer
freezes every other screen in the shop, and the till already locks stock while it
sells so two cashiers cannot both sell the same last packet. The backup was
rewritten at the same time: in WAL mode, copying the database file alone can
silently leave out the morning's takings.
